Financial Power vs Sporting Success: A 20-Year Analysis of the Turkish Super League

1. Project Overview and Scope

This project aims to analyze the relationship between financial strength and on-field performance of football clubs in the Turkish Super League over a long-term period. Football has increasingly become a data-driven industry where financial resources play a crucial role in determining competitive success. However, the extent to which financial power translates into sporting success remains an open question.

The primary objective of this study is to investigate whether higher squad values and financial indicators lead to better league performance. In addition to a single-season analysis, this project extends its scope to a longitudinal perspective by examining how clubs’ financial structures and performances have evolved over the past 20 years (from the 2004–2005 season to the 2024–2025 season).

More specifically, the project will address the following questions:

  • Is there a strong relationship between squad market value and league performance?
  • Which clubs outperform or underperform relative to their financial capacity?

By combining financial and performance datasets, this project aims to provide both descriptive and analytical insights into the efficiency and competitiveness of clubs in the Turkish football ecosystem.

2. Data

2.1 Data Source

The datasets used in this project are collected from two primary sources:

  • Transfermarkt/Startseite: Used to obtain financial and squad-related information such as total squad value, average player value, average age, squad size, and number of foreign players.
  • Transfermarkt/Tabelle: Used to collect official league performance statistics such as points, wins, draws, losses, goals scored, goals conceded, and league rankings.

The data has been collected through web scraping techniques and manually verified for consistency. The Python script used can be downloaded by clicking here.

The script is created thanks to Gemini 3.1 GenAI tool. You can refer to prompts used for creation of the script here.

2.2 General Information About Data

The project utilizes two main datasets covering a 20-season period from 2004–2005 to 2024–2025.

  • Dataset 1: Financial and Squad Data (superlig_kadro_verileri)

This dataset contains team-level financial and structural information for each season.

  • Dataset 2: League Performance Data (superlig_puan_durumu_verileri)

This dataset contains performance-related statistics for each team-season.

2.3 Reason of Choice

The choice of this dataset is motivated by several factors:

  • Relevance to Türkiye: The dataset focuses on the Turkish Super League, aligning with the course recommendation to use locally relevant data.
  • Richness and size: The dataset spans 20 seasons and includes multiple variables, making it suitable for in-depth analysis.
  • Real-world applicability: The relationship between financial investment and performance is a widely discussed topic in sports economics.
  • Availability of multiple dimensions: The dataset allows for combining financial, demographic, and performance variables.
  • Longitudinal analysis opportunity: The availability of long-term data enables trend analysis and structural comparisons across years.

This combination makes the dataset highly suitable for both exploratory and advanced analytical techniques.

3. Analysis

In this project, multiple analyses will be conducted to investigate the relationship between financial strength and sporting performance of clubs in the Turkish Super League.

The planned analyses are as follows:

  • Distribution Analysis: The distribution of squad values and performance indicators will be analyzed to understand overall league structure.
  • Correlation Analysis: The relationship between total squad market value and total points will be examined to understand whether financially stronger teams perform better.
  • Efficiency Analysis: Clubs will be evaluated based on how efficiently they convert financial resources into points.
  • Longitudinal Analysis (20-Year Club Evolution): The squad value and performance evolution of clubs over the past 20 years will be analyzed. This includes examining trends in squad value and performance consistency, as well as identifying clubs that improved or declined over time.

veri_analiz <- veri %>%
  group_by(Sezon) %>%
  mutate(
    Sezon_Ortalama_Piyasa_Degeri = mean(Toplam_Piyasa_Degeri_Euro, na.rm = TRUE),

Due to economic inflation over time and various factors related to the football industry, the purchasing power of 1 million euros in football in 2004 is not the same as that of 1 million euros in 2024. If we had simply taken squad market value figures into account, all teams from 2004 would have appeared “super efficient,” while today’s teams would have appeared “very inefficient.” For this reason, we looked at relative squad market value instead of squad market value. This value is calculated by dividing each team’s squad market value by the league’s average squad market value for that season. Additionally, since squad market value figures contain extreme variations, we examined the logarithmic transformation of relative squad market value figures to mitigate this extremity.

The number of teams in the league varied across some seasons. While the number of teams per season was mostly 18, in the season when relegation was suspended due to COVID-19 and subsequent seasons aligned with that format, the league featured 19, 20, or 21 teams. For this reason, we factored in points per game rather than total points.

A team that has only spent a few seasons in the Turkish Super League but managed to accumulate a decent number of points by securing 3–5 wins along the way (e.g., Pendikspor or Balıkesirspor) could have topped the “most efficient team” list due to mathematical anomalies. For this reason, to filter out “yoyo clubs,” we included the criterion of “teams that have competed in the Turkish Super League for at least 5 seasons.”

3.2.3 Interpreting correlation coefficients

Pearson correlation is the most commonly used statistic to measure the degree of the relationship between linearly related variables.

For the Pearson correlation, both variables should be normally distributed, since normally distributions exhibit a bell-shaped curve. Additionally, other key assumptions include linearity and homoscedasticity. Linearity assumes a straight line relationship between each of the two variables, while homoscedasticity assumes that data is equally distributed about the regression line.

Spearman correlation is a non-parametric test that measures the degree of association between two variables. Moreover, the Spearman rank correlation test does not carry any assumptions about the distribution of the data. It is the appropriate correlation analysis when one measure the variables on a scale that is at least ordinal.

For our context, Pearson correlation answers: “In each season, is there a linear relationship between PPG and log of relative squad market value?” and Spearman correlation answers: “In each season, are ranks of points collected and squad market value similar?”

  • When examining season-based correlations, it is clear that the relationship between squad market value and points performance is generally positive and strong. This suggests that teams with greater financial strength tend to accumulate more points in most seasons. However, differences between Pearson and Spearman correlations reveal that the nature of this relationship varies across seasons. In seasons where the Pearson correlation coefficient is significantly higher than the Spearman correlation coefficient, while the linear relationship between squad value and points is strong, the teams’ market value rankings do not fully align with their point standings. This can be explained by some teams performing better or worse than expected relative to their squad values.

  • Special case when Pearson >> Spearman (Examples: 2005-2006, 2017-2018 and 2023-2024)

    • There is a strong linear relationship between squad value and points; however, the squad value rankings and the points rankings are not aligned to the same extent. There are several possible reasons for this:

      • A few high-value teams may have accumulated very high points, thereby skewing the Pearson correlation upward.

      • In the middle-to-lower ranks, squad value and point rankings may be mixed up.

      • Two or three teams in the league may be significantly divergent in terms of both financial and sporting performance; this could boost the linear correlation while weakening the overall alignment of the rankings.

3.5 Key Takeaways

  • Squad market value and sporting performance are positively related. Across the analyzed seasons, teams with higher relative squad market values generally achieved higher points per match, indicating that financial strength is an important driver of Süper Lig performance. However, squad value alone does not fully explain success. The dispersion around the regression line shows that several clubs either overperformed or underperformed relative to what their squad value would suggest, highlighting the role of managerial quality, squad planning, stability, and other sporting factors.

  • Residual analysis provides a useful efficiency measure. Positive residuals identify clubs that converted their squad value into points more efficiently than expected, while negative residuals indicate underperformance relative to financial strength. This allows clubs to be compared beyond simple points or market value rankings.

  • Long-term club profiles differ significantly. While clubs such as Fenerbahçe, Galatasaray, and Beşiktaş combine high squad values with high points-per-match averages, Başakşehir stands out as a strong overperformer relative to its average squad value, whereas Trabzonspor appears below the expected performance line in the long-term comparison.

  • The longitudinal analysis shows that efficiency changes over time. Annual residual trends for the four major clubs reveal that performance relative to squad value is not constant; clubs move between overperformance and underperformance across seasons, suggesting that financial power must be continuously converted into sporting output.
